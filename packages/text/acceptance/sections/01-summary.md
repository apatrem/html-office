# Executive summary {#sec-summary .new-page}

Larkfield Bakery Co-operative had its strongest year so far. Revenue grew 14% to EUR 9.6 million and operating profit rose from EUR 430,000 to EUR 620,000, while the co-operative kept its promise to pay every baker above the regional living wage.[^audit] This review explains what drove the year and sets out the plan the members will vote on in December. {.lead}

> [!KEY]
> The 2027 plan opens a fourth bakehouse at Riverside, moves all baking to electric ovens by 2028 and holds operating profit at about 7% of revenue while prices rise less than costs.

Three things explain 2026. First, wholesale customers came back after two difficult years: cafés and school kitchens now take a fifth more bread than in 2025 ([[xref: sec-channels]]). Second, the online subscription box, launched as a pilot in 2024, became a real channel with over 1,400 weekly boxes by December. Third, the new production schedule at the Old Town bakehouse cut waste by a third and freed capacity for the autumn peak ([[xref: sec-operations]]).

Not everything went to plan. Flour prices rose again in the spring, energy costs stayed high until the new tariff started in September, and the Westgate shop lost money for the second year in a row. The board decided in July to reduce Westgate's opening hours rather than close it; the results of that change are discussed in [[xref: sec-risks]].

The plan for 2027 ([[xref: sec-plan]]) has four priorities:

1. Open the Riverside bakehouse in September, with an electric oven line and a shop.
2. Grow wholesale by another 15%, mostly with schools and hospital kitchens.
3. Keep online subscriptions growing without adding a second delivery van, by extending the cargo-bike loop.
4. Bring the Westgate shop back to break-even or decide on its future by the end of June.

The budget in [[xref: tab-budget]] expects revenue of EUR 10.7 million and operating profit of EUR 660,000, after EUR 120,000 of one-off opening costs. [[xref: fig-bridge]] shows how the plan moves from this year's profit to next year's. The capital programme, EUR 1 million over the year, is financed from cash and the co-operative's existing loan facility; no new member capital is required.

The board asks the members to approve the plan and the budget at the general meeting on 12 December 2026.

[^audit]: All figures in this document are fictional and unaudited. The co-operative and its sites are invented for the html-text acceptance document.
