# 2026 in review {#sec-review .new-page}

This chapter looks at the year from three sides: who bought from us and through which channel, how the bakeries and the delivery loop performed, and how the team changed. The financial results that follow from it are in [[xref: sec-finance]]. {.lead}

## Customers and channels {#sec-channels}

The co-operative sells through three channels. The four shops still bring in most of the revenue, but their share keeps falling as wholesale and online grow faster. [[xref: tab-revenue]] gives the figures by channel; [[xref: fig-revenue]] shows the same growth quarter by quarter.

[[block: chart | intent: "Revenue by quarter, 2025 and 2026" | id: fig-revenue]]

**Shops.** Revenue in the shops rose 10.6% to EUR 5.6 million. Mill Lane, the first and busiest shop, grew fastest, helped by longer Saturday hours and the new breakfast counter. Station Road and Hillcrest grew in line with the average. Westgate fell by 4% and remains the only shop that does not cover its costs.

Footfall in the shops was almost flat; the growth came from a higher average basket, which rose from EUR 7.80 to EUR 8.55. About half of that increase came from price rises in March and September, the other half from customers buying more: the breakfast counter, the larger sourdough loaf and the seasonal cakes all sold better than planned.

**Wholesale.** Cafés, restaurants and school kitchens bought EUR 2.9 million of bread and pastries, 20% more than in 2025. Two contracts made most of the difference: the city's school meals service, which moved twelve primary schools to Larkfield bread in February, and a regional chain of nine cafés that replaced its frozen pastries with ours in May.[^contracts] Wholesale margins are lower than shop margins, but wholesale orders are known a day ahead, which makes baking more efficient and reduces waste.

**Online and subscriptions.** The subscription box delivers a weekly selection of bread to homes and offices by cargo bike. Revenue doubled from EUR 0.45 million in 2025 to EUR 0.9 million in 2026 on an annualised basis, and the channel as a whole, including one-off online orders and click-and-collect, reached EUR 1.1 million. Cancellations stayed below 3% a month after the second-month offer was dropped in April.

> [!NOTE]
> Customer satisfaction, measured each quarter by a short survey in the shops and by e-mail for subscribers, stayed between 4.5 and 4.7 out of 5 all year. The most frequent complaint was the queue at Mill Lane on Saturday mornings.

## Bakeries and operations {#sec-operations}

[[block: image | intent: "Mill Lane shopfront" | id: fig-mill-lane]]

The co-operative bakes in three bakehouses: Old Town, which supplies the shops and the subscription boxes; the wholesale bakehouse at the Docks, which runs at night; and the small bakehouse behind Mill Lane, which bakes for that shop and for special orders. Together they baked about 4.1 million items in 2026, 11% more than in 2025.

The biggest operational change of the year was the new production schedule at Old Town. Until March, each shop ordered for the next day on the evening before, and the bakehouse baked to those orders plus a safety margin. Since April, orders are planned a week ahead from the sales history of each shop and corrected in the afternoon for weather, school holidays and local events. Unsold bread at closing fell from 9.4% of production to 6.1%, and the time bakers spend on late changes fell by half.

The wholesale bakehouse at the Docks reached 92% of its capacity in the autumn. Night shifts were extended twice to meet demand from the new café contracts, and two orders had to be refused in November. This is the main reason for the Riverside project in the 2027 plan ([[xref: sec-priorities]]).

[[block: image | intent: "The cargo-bike delivery loop" | id: fig-route]]

Deliveries to homes, offices and small cafés use four cargo bikes on a fixed loop that starts and ends at the Old Town bakehouse. In 2026 the loop was extended to the Docks and Riverside districts, and a second morning round was added on weekdays. The bikes covered about 38,000 kilometres; the one diesel van, kept for large wholesale orders, covered 21,000 kilometres, 30% less than in 2025.

Energy remains the most volatile cost. Gas still heats two of the three bakehouses. The fixed tariff signed in September cut the price per kilowatt-hour by a fifth for two years, but it does not remove the co-operative's exposure in the long run. The decision to move to electric ovens is explained in [[xref: sec-plan]].

## People {#sec-people}

The co-operative ended the year with 118 worker-members and 23 employees on trial periods or fixed-term contracts, up from 104 and 19 a year earlier. Thirty-five people joined during the year and seventeen left, a turnover of 13%, lower than the 22% of 2025 and well below the average for the trade in the region.[^turnover]

Every baker and shop worker is paid at least 8% above the regional living wage, as the co-operative's rules require. The general meeting in June approved a 4.5% increase in the base rate from 1 July, slightly above inflation. Night work at the Docks is paid a 25% premium.

Training took 2,900 hours, about twenty-five hours per person. Most of it was the new two-year apprenticeship in bread and viennoiserie, run with the city's vocational college, which six apprentices started in September. Two senior bakers trained as instructors so that the programme can take eight apprentices a year from 2027.

Safety improved: the number of reportable incidents fell from five to two, both minor burns. The new extraction hoods at Old Town, installed in January, brought flour dust levels well below the legal limit.

## Suppliers and sustainability {#sec-sustainability}

The co-operative buys from about forty suppliers. The ten largest account for 80% of purchases, led by the two mills that supply all our wheat and rye flour. Both mills are within 120 kilometres and buy their grain from farms in the region; one of them has been a supplier since the first Mill Lane bakery opened.

Three commitments guide purchasing, and the members review them every year:

- **Flour from the region, organic where it is available.** In 2026, 60% of the wheat flour and all the rye flour were organic, against 55% and 100% in 2025. The share of organic wheat flour is limited by what the two mills can supply at a price the shops can pass on.
- **No air freight and no palm oil.** Chocolate, vanilla and coffee are the only ingredients that come from outside Europe; all three are bought from co-operatives with a fair-trade certification.
- **Packaging that can be recycled or composted.** The last plastic windows disappeared from paper bags in May ([[xref: sec-costs]]), and the subscription boxes are collected and reused about eight times on average.

The carbon footprint of the co-operative was estimated for the first time in 2026, with the help of a student from the city's university.[^footprint] Flour and other ingredients make up about half of it, energy for baking about a third, and deliveries less than a tenth. The figures are uncertain, but they confirm that the move to electric ovens ([[xref: sec-plan]]) and the choice of suppliers matter far more than the delivery fleet.

Unsold bread is a cost and a waste. The shops sell the day's remaining bread at half price in the last hour, and what is left goes to two food banks and a farm that feeds it to pigs. In 2026 the food banks received about 21 tonnes of bread, two-thirds of what they received in 2025, because the new production schedule left less over.

## Community {#sec-community}

The co-operative's rules set aside 2% of the surplus each year for community work, decided by the members. In 2026 that came to about EUR 8,000, to which the shops added bread, cakes and time.

- **School baking sessions.** Bakers ran thirty-two morning sessions in the twelve schools of the meals contract, where pupils made and took home their own loaves. Teachers asked for more sessions in 2027, and the plan includes forty.
- **Food banks.** Besides the unsold bread already mentioned, the co-operative paid for a second freezer at the Westgate food bank so that it can keep bread over the weekend.
- **Training for people out of work.** Together with the city's employment service, the Old Town bakehouse offered four six-week placements. Two of the four people are now on the apprenticeship.
- **The harvest market.** Mill Lane hosted the district's harvest market in September for the third year. About 2,500 people came, and the stalls of twenty local producers sold out.

These activities are small in money but matter to the members, many of whom joined the co-operative because of them. Several customers wrote in the autumn survey that the school sessions were the reason they first came to a Larkfield shop.

[^footprint]: The estimate follows a simplified method for small food businesses and covers ingredients, energy, deliveries, packaging and waste. It is not certified.

[^contracts]: The school meals contract runs for three years from February 2026; the café chain's contract for one year, renewable each May.

[^turnover]: Turnover counts members and employees who left during the year, divided by the average headcount.
