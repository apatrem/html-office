# Plan for 2027 {#sec-plan .new-page}

The plan builds on what worked in 2026 and addresses the two constraints the year exposed: baking capacity for wholesale and dependence on gas. It was prepared by the board with the shop, bakehouse and delivery teams between September and November, and discussed at two open members' evenings. {.lead}

## Priorities {#sec-priorities}

**1. Open the Riverside bakehouse.** The co-operative has signed a ten-year lease on a former print works at Riverside, five minutes by bike from the Docks. The building will house a wholesale bakehouse with an electric oven line ([[xref: fig-oven]]), a small shop and a covered depot for the cargo bikes. Works start in March; baking starts in September. Riverside will take over a third of the wholesale volume from the Docks, so that the Docks can stop its extended night shifts and take on new contracts again.

[[block: image | intent: "The electric oven line planned for Riverside" | id: fig-oven]]

The investment is EUR 780,000: EUR 420,000 for the ovens, mixers and cooling, EUR 290,000 for building works and EUR 70,000 for the shop. One-off opening costs, mainly training and overlapping rent, are EUR 120,000 and are included in the 2027 budget.

**2. Grow wholesale by 15%, with more customers.** The target is EUR 3.3 million of wholesale revenue, with at least three new customers in schools, hospitals and workplace canteens, so that no single customer is above 8% of revenue by December. The sales team will grow from one to two people in January.

**3. Grow subscriptions without a second van.** The plan expects 1,800 weekly boxes by December. Two more cargo bikes and the Riverside depot will extend the loop to Hillcrest and the Station district. Deliveries will move to two fixed windows a day, which customers asked for in the autumn survey.

**4. Decide on Westgate.** The shop will keep its shorter hours and test a lunch offer from January to April. The board will present the first-half figures and three options to the members in June.

## Budget {#sec-budget}

[[block: table | intent: "2027 budget by quarter" | id: tab-budget]]

The budget in [[xref: tab-budget]] assumes revenue of EUR 10.7 million, up 11.5%. Shops grow 5% (mostly prices), wholesale 15% and online 20%. Operating costs rise 11.8% to EUR 10.0 million, including EUR 120,000 of opening costs for Riverside, a 3.5% pay rise in July, and flour prices 5% above the 2026 average.

[[block: chart | intent: "From 2026 operating profit to the 2027 plan" | id: fig-bridge]]

Operating profit is planned at EUR 660,000, 6.2% of revenue. Without the one-off opening costs it would be EUR 780,000, or 7.3%, above the members' target. [[xref: fig-bridge]] shows the main movements: higher volumes and prices add EUR 530,000; flour, energy and wages take away EUR 370,000; and Riverside's opening costs EUR 120,000.

Capital spending, outside the operating budget, is EUR 1.0 million: Riverside (EUR 780,000), two cargo bikes, the replacement of the Mill Lane counter and the new card terminals. It is financed by EUR 600,000 from cash and EUR 400,000 drawn on the existing loan facility. Cash is expected to stay above EUR 500,000 throughout the year, with its lowest point in October, after the last Riverside payment.

> [!WARNING]
> The budget is most sensitive to flour prices and to the Riverside opening date. Flour 10% above the assumption would cost about EUR 160,000; a three-month delay at Riverside would reduce operating profit by about EUR 90,000, as the Docks would keep its extended shifts.

## People in 2027 {#sec-plan-people}

Riverside needs about twenty-two people when it opens: fourteen bakers and bakery assistants, five people for the shop and three for the delivery loop and the depot. About half will come from the Docks, where the night shifts return to their normal length, and the rest will be recruited from May. New colleagues start with a six-month trial period, as the co-operative's rules require, and can apply to become members after it.

The apprenticeship programme will take eight apprentices in September, two of them for Riverside. The two instructors trained in 2026 will share the teaching with a baker from the vocational college. The board also proposes a short course in shop management for the four shop leaders, who asked for it in the staff survey.

The budget includes a 3.5% increase in the base rate from 1 July, in line with expected inflation, and keeps the 8% margin above the regional living wage. The night premium at the Docks and Riverside stays at 25%. A working group of members will propose by March how the patronage refund should treat part-time work, which several members raised at the June meeting.

> [!NOTE]
> Recruitment is the main practical risk for Riverside. The board has agreed with the vocational college that the apprentices who finish their first year in June can choose to work at Riverside from September.

## Milestones and governance {#sec-milestones}

The board will report progress to the members each quarter, with the figures by shop and by channel, using the same tables as this review. The main milestones are:

- January: second salesperson joins; Westgate lunch test starts.
- March: Riverside building works start; the members receive the first-quarter report in April.
- June: decision on Westgate at the general meeting; half-year accounts.
- September: Riverside bakehouse and shop open; the Docks returns to normal night shifts.
- December: general meeting, results for 2027 and plan for 2028, including the Old Town oven replacement.

The plan will be revised if wholesale orders fall more than 10% below budget for two months in a row, or if Riverside's opening slips beyond November. In either case the board will propose changes to the members within a month.
