# Financial results {#sec-finance .new-page}

Revenue grew faster than costs for the first time since 2022, and operating profit rose by 44%. The margin, 6.5% of revenue, is still below the 7% the members set as a target in 2024, but the gap has halved. {.lead}

## Revenue {#sec-revenue}

[[block: table | intent: "Revenue by channel" | id: tab-revenue]]

Total revenue was EUR 9.6 million, up EUR 1.2 million or 14.3% on 2025. Price rises explain about a third of the growth: the shops raised prices by an average of 3% in March and 2% in September, and wholesale prices were indexed on flour costs in January as the contracts allow. The rest is volume, mostly from the two new wholesale contracts and from subscriptions.

The quarterly pattern in [[xref: fig-revenue]] is familiar: a quiet first quarter, then steady growth to the December peak. What changed is the size of the step from the third to the fourth quarter, smaller than in earlier years because wholesale, which has little seasonality, now makes up a larger part of the total.

Revenue per worker-member rose from EUR 80,800 to EUR 81,400. This is a modest increase, since much of the new business is wholesale, which needs more hours per euro of revenue than the shops. It is still the best figure in the co-operative's history.

## Costs and operating profit {#sec-costs}

Operating costs rose 12.7% to EUR 9.0 million ([[xref: tab-costs]]). Wages and training are the largest line at 37% of revenue, followed by ingredients and packaging at 33%.

[[block: table | intent: "Operating costs and profit" | id: tab-costs]]

**Ingredients and packaging** cost EUR 3.2 million, 18% more than in 2025. Volume explains about two-thirds of the increase and flour prices the rest: organic wheat flour, which makes up 60% of the flour we buy, cost 9% more on average than in 2025. Butter prices fell slightly. The switch to paper bags without plastic windows, completed in May, saved EUR 14,000 a year.

**Wages and training** rose 11% to EUR 3.6 million, with more people, the July pay rise and the night premium at the Docks. As a share of revenue, wages fell from 38.0% to 37.0%.

**Energy** cost EUR 510,000, 21% more than in 2025, because the old tariff ran until September and the wholesale bakehouse ran longer hours. The new tariff should bring the cost back to about EUR 450,000 in 2027 at the same volume.

**Rent and sites** rose only 5%, as two leases were renewed at unchanged rents. **Distribution** rose 10%, less than the volume delivered, thanks to the cargo bikes. **Other overheads**, including insurance, accounting, the card terminals and the website, were held flat.

Operating profit was EUR 620,000, 6.5% of revenue, against EUR 430,000 and 5.1% in 2025. After interest on the equipment loan and tax, the net result is a surplus of EUR 410,000.[^surplus] The board proposes to keep 70% of the surplus in reserves and to pay 30% to members as a patronage refund in proportion to hours worked.

> [!WARNING]
> Cash fell to EUR 680,000 in August, the lowest point of the year, when the energy bill, the summer holiday pay and the Docks oven repair fell in the same month. The 2027 budget keeps a minimum of EUR 500,000 and moves the Riverside payments to avoid August.

## Cash and investment {#sec-cash}

The co-operative generated EUR 760,000 of cash from operations in 2026, against EUR 540,000 in 2025. It spent EUR 410,000 on equipment and works, repaid EUR 150,000 of the equipment loan and paid EUR 96,000 of patronage refunds for 2025. Cash at the end of October was EUR 1.12 million, and the forecast for 31 December is EUR 1.25 million, after the December peak.

The main investments of the year were:

- the extraction hoods and a second spiral mixer at Old Town (EUR 140,000);
- the repair of the Docks deck oven and its new control system (EUR 95,000), which was not planned;
- two cargo bikes and the first part of the bike depot at the Docks (EUR 60,000);
- the new online shop and subscription system (EUR 55,000);
- smaller replacements in the four shops (EUR 60,000).

The equipment loan, taken in 2023 to fit out the Docks bakehouse, stands at EUR 600,000 and is repaid over five more years. The co-operative also has an unused credit facility of EUR 500,000 with its bank, which the plan for 2027 uses in part ([[xref: sec-budget]]). Members' capital, EUR 1,180 per worker-member, did not change during the year.

Debtors are low because most sales are paid at the till or online in advance. Wholesale customers pay at thirty days; at the end of October they owed EUR 310,000, none of it more than sixty days late. Stock is small and turns over every few days, except flour, of which the co-operative keeps about three weeks in silos at the Docks to protect against delivery problems.

> [!NOTE]
> The board will propose at the December meeting to set the minimum cash balance at EUR 500,000, about three weeks of operating costs, and to report it each quarter with the figures by shop.

[^surplus]: Interest of EUR 45,000 and corporation tax of EUR 165,000. The surplus figure is before the patronage refund.
